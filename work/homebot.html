The problem

Most agents lose touch with clients within months of closing.

Not because clients are disloyal, but because the relationship ends with the transaction. Homeownership is a long-term financial journey, and the advisors who stay useful through it are the ones who get the repeat business and referrals.

Homebot sends homeowners a personalized digest about their home's value, their market, and their mortgage, and tells their agent or loan officer when it's a good moment to reach out.

My role

Senior Product Designer, owning the product experience from discovery through delivery for mobile and responsive web.

  • Designed the homeowner digest and dashboard, and turned complicated financial data into visuals people could act on
  • Ran research, usability testing, and behavior analysis with real users
  • Built the personas and flows that informed product strategy
  • Shaped direction with the Product Director, PM, and Tech Lead, and worked with engineering, CX, and marketing

Every quarter we ran the same cycle: identify problem spaces, prioritize by impact, right-size the scope, plan and run research, synthesize, and deliver solutions tied to business goals and user value.

Design for Felicia

We had four personas, and designed for the least confident one first.

Passive Pete needs nudges. Powerhouse Paul is a top producer who wants ROI fast. Motivated Miranda is growth-minded and already uses the tools. Flustered Felicia has been in the industry less than three years, is overwhelmed, and is slow to adopt new tech.

The principle: if we can build for Felicia, Paul and Miranda will still adopt, Pete will stay engaged, and Felicia won't churn. Designing for the least confident user made the product easier for everyone.

The homeowner digest

Homeownership builds wealth, but the finance behind it feels confusing, transactional, and intimidating. The digest aimed to make it easy, actionable, and personal:

  • Home value and equity: a live estimate, so "my investment is working" feels real
  • Market and sell signals: is it a good time to sell, and what would I walk away with?
  • Next home: what could I afford today, with no need to leave for Zillow
  • Refinance and PMI: what paying a little more would save, and when mortgage insurance can come off

Every view and click also became a signal for the advisor.

The advisor dashboard

Advisors couldn't tell which clients to call, or when.

The goal was to make it effortless for agents and loan officers to spot warm leads and act at the right moment. The dashboard ranks the most active homeowners, buyers, and potential sellers based on what they've engaged with that week.

Research Council

Research kept happening inside one project and then vanishing. So I started a weekly meeting where it couldn't.

I created and ran a weekly Research Council, attended mostly by product managers and designers. It gave teams a standing place to share what they were hearing from customers, talk through research in progress, and compare insights across problem areas, so one team's findings could inform another team's work.

Kickoff frameworks

The frameworks had one job: help teams agree on the problem before jumping into solutions.

I created a research kickoff template and an approach called "Right-Sizing Your Problem" to give discovery more structure. Both asked the same questions up front: what we were trying to learn, how big the problem actually was, and how much research it warranted. They were built for product managers and designers working together at the start of a project.

An evolved version became the Design Kick-off Doc I publish for other designers.

Mentorship program

I started the Homebot Mentorship Program to give people across the company a light, structured way to learn from each other and work toward their professional goals.

I designed how it worked: matching mentors and mentees, setting goals, agreeing on expectations, and giving each pair enough guidance to make the relationship useful without turning it into bureaucracy. About 12 people took part, and it ran quarter after quarter.

I can't point to promotions or retention numbers, and I won't claim them. What I can say is that I took it from an idea to a program people kept signing up for.

I tend to step into ambiguous situations

and create enough clarity and structure for people to move forward.

What I learned

  • Education fuels empowerment, and empowered users take action.
  • Timing plus relevance is what converts. Surface the right insight at the right moment, and outreach gets easier.
  • Design shapes the problem space, and can drive product strategy and adoption, not just screens.

It's the same instinct behind my work at Pax8: I'm not the person who always has the answer. I'm the person who makes the problem clear enough for a team to find one.
