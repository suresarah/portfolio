Pax8 · Voyager Alliance · 2025

Pax8's last rewards program died because Pax8 paid for all of it.

I led the research and design for its replacement: a three-sided rewards system where partners earn, vendors fund, and Pax8 runs the whole loop.

My role
Senior Product Designer. Led research, product and design strategy, and UX/UI.
Worked with
Product, Finance, Vendor Ops, Engineering, Marketing
Designed
Partner dashboard, rewards marketplace, redemption, admin tooling, vendor participation
Launch
Rolled out at Beyond 2025. About 70% of eligible partners opted in during the first week.

Context

Pax8 is a cloud marketplace in the middle of two groups. Managed service providers (MSPs) buy, sell, and manage cloud software through it. Software vendors rely on it to reach those MSPs. Pax8 only grows if both sides do.

Competitors were launching loyalty programs, and vendors were shifting from direct sales to marketplaces. Pax8 needed a rewards program that would bring partners back, and it had tried before.

Why the last one failed

The previous program was one-sided. Only partners earned points, and Pax8 funded every reward.

That had a chain of consequences: there was little to earn and little worth redeeming, so engagement stayed low. Nothing set it apart from competitors. And without vendor funding it couldn't scale, so it was discontinued.

The direction I was given was to build a new partner loyalty program. The brief included vendor-funded rewards. My job was to figure out what the program should actually be and how that model would work for the three groups who'd use it.

What partners wanted

Before designing, I ran a 72-person survey of people at MSPs. It included both Pax8 and non-Pax8 partners, all familiar with programs like TD Synnex Hyve, Ingram Micro Xvantage, Microsoft's partner incentives, and Amex business cards. I also audited those programs and others from Dell and Microsoft.

Partners liked programs that were easy to earn from and easy to spend. They tuned out narrow, vendor-specific perks.

The programs partners rated highest balanced two things: quick, frequent wins ("points dump" programs like Amex and Dell) and rewards that helped their business grow, like training, enablement, and support services. Concierge-style perks and single-vendor rewards felt irrelevant.

There was a second finding about context. Partners run in what one summary called "organized chaos," using anywhere from 4 to 20 tools a day. Pax8 is one of many. If the program took effort to understand, it would get ignored.

A caveat I kept in the work: the partner research panel represents about 2% of Pax8 partner users, and people who opt into research tend to be similar to each other. We treated the findings as direction, not certainty.

Key decisions

Sarah: confirm each decision was yours and what the alternatives were. Replace or cut any that weren't.

1. One flexible marketplace instead of vendor-specific perks

ConsideredRewards tied to individual vendors, which is how many competitor programs work.
ChoseA single marketplace with broad categories: cashback, services, training, and tools.

The survey was clear that narrow perks get ignored. A single marketplace also gives vendors a reason to participate, since their rewards are seen by every partner, not just their own customers.

Rewards marketplace + redemption flow. Proves the decision became real UI.

2. Three ways for vendors to participate

ConsideredRequire every vendor to both fund rewards and offer redemption.
ChoseThree models: earn-only, redeem-only, and full loop.

The old program failed because vendor funding never came through. Lowering the commitment for vendors made the funding model more likely to hold.

3. Show partners exactly how they earned

ConsideredA points balance and a catalog.
ChoseA dashboard that shows accruals, point types, tier progress, and redemptions.

The research pointed to four needs: clarity (what do I get?), simplicity (don't make me dig), and transparency (how did I earn this?), and flexibility (let me choose). For a program competing with 4 to 20 other tools for attention, clarity was the feature.

The system

Voyager runs on three surfaces, one for each group.

  • Partners: track accruals and tier progress, browse rewards, and redeem.
  • Pax8 admins: approve or opt out partners, adjust points (including in bulk), and monitor program health and financials.
  • Vendors: fund rewards, create offers, and see the return.

Edge cases mattered because points are money. I designed for cancellations, returns, and partial redemptions, so accruals stayed accurate when real orders changed.

Launch & after

Voyager rolled out at Beyond 2025. About 70% of eligible partners opted in during the first week, and partners were redeeming rewards right away.
