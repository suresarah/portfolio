Pax8 · Voyager Alliance · 2025

Pax8's last rewards program died because Pax8 paid for all of it.

The replacement is a tiered program that vendors help fund. The business rules were complicated, so my job was making them understandable: where a partner stands, what to do next, and what they unlock.

My role
Senior Product Designer. Led research and the design of the partner, admin, and vendor experiences.
Worked with
Product, Finance, Vendor Ops, Engineering, Marketing
Designed
Pre-opt-in and enrollment, tier progression, eligibility states, partner dashboard, redemption, admin tooling
Shipped
MVP launched alongside Beyond 2025

Context

Pax8 is a cloud marketplace that sits between two groups. Managed service providers (MSPs) buy, sell, and manage cloud software through it, and software vendors rely on it to reach those MSPs. Pax8 only grows if both sides do.

Competitors were launching loyalty programs, and Pax8 had tried one before.

Why the last one failed

The previous program was one-sided. Only partners earned points, and Pax8 funded every reward.

That meant little to earn, little worth redeeming, and low engagement. Nothing set it apart from competitors, and without vendor funding it couldn't scale. It was discontinued.

What was mine

The business set the direction: a new loyalty program, funded in part by vendors, with tiers based on monthly spend. The tier thresholds came from the program model, not from me.

My part was everything a partner sees and does: how they understand their tier, what qualifies them for the next one, what changes when they move up or down, and what happens when they become eligible or drop out.

I also led the research behind it and designed the admin tools Pax8 uses to run the program.

Research

I ran a 72-person survey of people at MSPs, including partners who don't use Pax8. All of them knew programs like TD Synnex Hyve, Ingram Micro Xvantage, Microsoft's partner incentives, or Amex business cards. I also audited those programs and Dell's.

Partners liked programs that were easy to earn from and easy to spend. They tuned out narrow, vendor-specific perks.

The highest-rated programs combined quick, frequent wins with rewards that help the business grow, like training, enablement, and support services. Partners also run in "organized chaos," juggling anywhere from 4 to 20 tools a day. Pax8 is one of many, so if the program took effort to understand, it would get ignored.

In December usability testing, every participant found Voyager Alliance from the left navigation, and 55% preferred keeping the "Voyager Alliance Rewards Program" name.

A caveat I kept in the work: the partner research panel is about 2% of Pax8 partner users, and people who opt into research tend to be alike. We treated findings as direction, not certainty.

Key decisions

1. Turn the tier rules into "spend → qualify → earn"

The rulesFour spend tiers, each with its own bonuses, multipliers, and eligibility conditions.
What partners seeWhere they are now, what to do next, and what they unlock when they get there.

Partners don't need the whole rulebook. They need their next step. Ignite became the threshold that matters in the experience, because $500 a month in eligible spend is where a partner starts earning points and rewards.

2. Design for every state, not just the happy path

Simple versionOne tier dashboard for partners who qualify.
What I designedSeparate states for the three-month welcome period, eligible, approaching eligibility, downgraded, and below Ignite.

The hardest one was a partner dropping below Ignite. Earning new points pauses, but points they've already earned can't look like they've disappeared. In a program built on trust, a balance that seems to vanish is the fastest way to lose a partner.

Export from Figma: the below-Ignite or downgraded state, ideally beside the eligible state. Proves edge-case thinking. This is the screen that best shows judgment.

3. Make the value clear before asking anyone to join

Typical approachA marketing page with a "Join" button.
What I designedA pre-opt-in experience where partners compare tiers, see requirements and benefits, read FAQs and legal terms, and preview the rewards catalog.

The goal was informed enrollment, not the most clicks. A partner who joins without understanding the program becomes a confused partner later, and a support ticket.

Running the program

Voyager has three groups of users. Partners track progress, browse rewards, and redeem. Pax8 admins approve or opt out partners, adjust points (individually or in bulk), and set up rewards. Vendors fund rewards and offer them.

Points are money, so edge cases on the order side mattered too: cancellations, returns, and partial redemptions all had to keep balances accurate.

Launch & after

The MVP launched alongside Beyond 2025. We set two success criteria for the pilot: 70% of eligible partners opting in and 10% redeeming a reward.
